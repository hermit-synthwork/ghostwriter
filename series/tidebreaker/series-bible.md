# TIDEBREAKER — series bible (DRAFT, awaiting approval)

A serialized mecha anime told one Instagram carousel at a time. Every episode
follows the same rookie pilot and the same patched-up machine through one
continuing season. English in the speech bubbles; Chinese and Japanese on the
subtitle strip.

- Title: TIDEBREAKER · 破浪者 · タイドブレイカー
- Genre mix: mech action drama, with a comic-relief episode roughly one week in four
  and small comic beats inside the drama.
- Rating: PG-13. Action is loud, bloodless and machine-on-machine.

---

## Logline

In a flooded harbour city held together by storm walls, a nineteen-year-old
salvage-yard dockhand is handed the controls of the oldest, most-patched
defence frame on the waterfront — just as the abandoned drones of a forgotten
defence network start answering someone's call.

## World

**Kaigan Port** — a near-future coastal megacity rebuilt on top of its own
drowned districts. Rising seas swallowed the old shoreline; the city survives
behind the **Storm Wall**, a forty-kilometre sea barrier of concrete, steel and
salvage. Canal streets, stacked neon housing, fish markets on floating
platforms, cranes everywhere. Typhoon season is half the year.

**Frames** — piloted walking machines, roughly 18 metres tall. They were built
for heavy salvage and wall repair, and they are the only thing big enough to
stand against Husks. Frames are industrial, chunky and repairable: exposed
hydraulics, riveted plates, mismatched replacement parts, work lights, winches
and harpoon cables. They look like dockyard equipment that learned to fight,
not like sleek war machines.

**Husks** — the enemy. Unmanned, faceless drones left behind by the **Old
Grid**, a coastal defence network abandoned decades ago. They crawl out of the
sea crusted in barnacles and rust, all sensor clusters and grasping limbs. There
is no one inside them. Destroying a Husk is never killing a person, so fights
can be big and still stay PG-13.

**Who holds the waterfront**
- **Tanabe Salvage** — a failing family salvage yard with one working frame.
  This is home.
- **Kanzaki Marine Security (KMS)** — a polished corporation with a fleet of
  modern frames, paid by the city to guard the Storm Wall. Rich, efficient,
  and hiding something.
- **Harbour Authority** — overworked city officials who issue salvage claims
  and hate paperwork.

## Fixed facts (never contradict these)

1. Hana pilots **Ashgull** in every episode where there is frame action. She
   never switches to another frame for good.
2. Ashgull's cockpit is cramped, with a cracked-then-taped side monitor and a
   small dashboard screen where PIPPA's simple face appears.
3. Husks are always unmanned. No human is ever killed or shown injured; pilots
   get rattled, bruised egos, soaked uniforms and wrecked equipment.
4. Tanabe Salvage is always short of money. Damage has consequences: repair
   bills, borrowed parts, owed favours.
5. Yui Kanzaki is a rival first, never a villain. She has her own code.
6. PIPPA is a salvaged, slightly glitchy onboard AI. She is helpful, literal,
   and very bad at timing.
7. Frames need a pilot inside; nothing in this world is remote-controlled
   except Husks.
8. Weather is a character: rain, wind, typhoon warnings and harbour fog shape
   every sortie.

## Main cast

**Hana Kurose** (黑濑花 · 黒瀬ハナ) — 19, the pilot. A dockhand who has watched
frames work her whole life and memorised every manual. Stubborn, quick,
over-apologises, then does the reckless thing anyway. She wants to prove the
old yard still matters. Flaw: takes on everyone's problems alone.

**Gen Tanabe** (田边源 · 田辺ゲン) — 56, owner and chief mechanic of Tanabe
Salvage, Hana's gruff mentor. Speaks in short sentences, hides kindness behind
repair invoices, and treats Ashgull like a stubborn old dog. Comic engine:
posted rules about not denting the frame that Hana keeps breaking.

**PIPPA** (皮帕 · ピッパ) — Ashgull's onboard AI, pulled out of a scrapped
ferry. Appears as a simple two-dot-and-line face on the dashboard screen.
Cheerful, literal, quotes safety statistics at the worst moments. Comic relief
inside the drama.

**Yui Kanzaki** (神崎唯 · 神崎ユイ) — 20, KMS's ace pilot and heir to the
company. Precise, proud, allergic to mess. Starts by stealing Hana's salvage
claim. Slowly discovers her family's company wants the Old Grid for itself.

## Frames

**Ashgull** (灰鸥 · アッシュガル) — Hana's frame. A decades-old harbour-defence
frame rebuilt from salvage. Chunky and broad-shouldered, dull grey plating with
faded safety-orange stripes on the forearms, one mismatched replacement
shoulder plate, a single wide visor slit with a soft amber glow, a winch and
harpoon cable on the right forearm, work floodlights on the chest. Moves heavy
and deliberate; when it runs, it thunders.

**Halcyon** (翠鸥 · ハルシオン) — Yui's KMS frame. Newer, slimmer and faster.
Clean white-and-teal plating, a smooth rounded head with twin horizontal sensor
lines, a folding shield arm and a pneumatic lance. Always spotless — until it
isn't.

## Look and design rules

- Original designs only. No V-fins, horn crests, chest vents or face grilles
  borrowed from any existing mecha franchise.
- No emblems, faction logos, insignia, unit numbers, kanji or lettering painted
  on frames, uniforms, walls or signs. Colour and shape tell factions apart.
- Frames look industrial and repairable. Damage is shown as dents, sparks,
  steam and loose cables — never as anything organic.
- Pilot suits are practical work gear: Hana's is a faded orange-and-grey
  flight suit with taped knees; Yui's is a fitted white-and-teal KMS suit.

## Season One arc (12 beats, one per episode)

1. **The Last Pilot Quits** — A storm-wall breach; Ashgull's pilot walks out.
   Hana climbs in without permission and holds the wall. Cliffhanger: the
   Harbour Authority wants to know who was piloting.
2. **Rules of the Yard** *(comic relief)* — Gen's list of rules; PIPPA boots
   up for the first time and immediately lists Hana's violations.
3. **Fish Market Swarm** — Husks swarm a floating market. Hana saves it, and
   drops a crane into the harbour. The repair bill arrives.
4. **The Claim Jumper** — Yui and Halcyon arrive and take the salvage claim
   Tanabe Salvage needed to survive the month.
5. **Eighteen Metres, No Parking** *(comic relief)* — Hana tries to run one
   ordinary errand in Ashgull. It does not go well.
6. **A Signal Inside** — Hana finds an Old Grid beacon inside a wrecked Husk.
   The drones aren't wandering. Something is calling them.
7. **Uneasy Allies** — Hana and Yui are forced to shut down a relay tower
   together, arguing the whole way up.
8. **What KMS Wants** — Yui overhears her company planning to seize the
   beacon, not destroy it.
9. **Typhoon Night** *(comic relief)* — A typhoon traps Hana, Gen, PIPPA and a
   very reluctant Yui in the hangar overnight.
10. **Scrap and Steel** — Ashgull is badly wrecked. Gen rebuilds it from
    everything left in the yard. It comes back uglier and stronger.
11. **Yui Chooses** — Yui turns Halcyon against her own company's plan.
12. **Hold the Wall** — Season finale: every frame on the Storm Wall. Hana
    shuts down the Old Grid core. Cliffhanger: a second, newer signal answers
    from far out at sea.

After beat 12, the writer continues into Season Two using the same cast and
fixed facts, starting from the second signal.

## Episode shape

- 6–8 panels. Panel 1 is a hook; from episode 2 onward it may open on a quick
  "previously" beat.
- The last panel lands a turn or a cliffhanger. An episode doesn't have to
  resolve the season's story.
- At most two lines of dialogue per panel. Narration sits at the top of the
  panel; the bottom band is reserved for subtitles.
- At most one one-off guest character per episode (a dock worker, a Harbour
  Authority clerk, a kid who follows frames).

## Comic-relief rules

- About one episode in four is a comic breather, flagged in the arc above.
- Comedy comes from character: Gen's rules, PIPPA's timing, Hana's
  over-apologising, Yui's horror at mess, a giant machine doing small chores.
- Comic episodes still move one small thread of the season forward.
- Inside drama episodes, allow one light beat, never mocking the stakes.

## Tone

Earnest and warm: found family on a failing waterfront. Big weather, big
machines, small human moments. Sacrifice means lost money, lost pride and wrecked
steel, never lost lives. Nobody is purely evil except the cold logic of the Old
Grid.

## Banned

- Mechs, characters, names, factions or plots that resemble existing mecha
  franchises (including Gundam, Evangelion, Macross, Pacific Rim,
  BattleTech/MechWarrior and Code Geass).
- Kaiju or organic giant monsters. Husks are machines.
- Blood, wounds, gore, human deaths, weapons aimed at people.
- Readable text, logos or insignia in the art.
- Real brands, real cities, real people.
