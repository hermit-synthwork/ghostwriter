# Ghostwriter house style — mecha-anime (FROZEN)

Full-colour TV mecha anime illustration. Clean confident lineart with cel
shading — two-tone shadows with a hard terminator and crisp specular glints on
metal — a rain-soaked harbour palette of steel blue, slate grey and deep navy
lit by sodium-orange work lights, cyan screen glow and neon reflections on wet
concrete. Piloted frames are about 18 metres tall and read as heavy industrial
machines: riveted plates, exposed hydraulics, cables, winches, work floodlights,
scuffs, dents and mismatched replacement parts. Show scale with cranes, storm
walls, canals and tiny human figures below. Action is kinetic and weighty —
low angles, splashing water, sparks, steam, speed lines and dust — and cockpit
shots are cramped and tactile with glowing monitors and taped-up panels.
Detailed where the eye lands (faces, hands, the frame's head and arms),
simplified painterly rendering in the distance. Expressive anime faces with
large eyes and clear emotion.

Tone: earnest, warm, high-stakes mecha drama with room for small human comedy —
big weather, big machines, small moments. Action is bloodless: machines dent,
spark, steam and fall apart; people get soaked, rattled and embarrassed, never
wounded. Warm-hearted, never mean-spirited; PG-13.

Characters: recurring cast and frames must match their model sheets exactly.
Frames are original industrial designs — no V-fins, horn crests, chest vents,
face grilles or silhouettes borrowed from any existing mecha franchise. No
emblems, faction logos, insignia, unit numbers, kanji or lettering painted on
frames, uniforms, walls, screens or signs; colour and shape alone tell factions
apart. Enemy drones are faceless unmanned machines crusted with barnacles and
rust — never organic monsters. Original figures only; no real anime or comics,
studios, franchises, or characters.

Hard rules for panel art:
- Every image is ONE single continuous illustration that runs edge to edge on all
  four sides — never draw comic panel borders, frame lines, gutters, inset panels,
  split or stacked panels, letterbox bars, or flat colour strips.
- No lettering, speech balloons, caption boxes, or readable signage in the
  image. Those are composited later in code.
- Keep faces and key action inside the central vertical 80%.
- Let the scene itself stay quiet near the top and bottom edges — open sky, rain,
  wet floor, water or plain wall that belongs to the same scene — because text is
  laid over those areas later.
- Exception: an explicitly requested hand-drawn SFX word may be integrated.
